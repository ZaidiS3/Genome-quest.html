🩺 What does it mean?

In a research study, this variant appeared more often in people with a particular disease than in healthy comparison people. What should the scientist report?

💡 Why?

An association is a clue: it shows the variant and the disease turn up together more than chance would explain. It doesn't prove cause, and it can't diagnose one person. That takes more evidence and validated clinical testing.

🔬 In the real world

Real reads are about 150 letters long and a sequencing run produces millions of them. Software aligns them all and flags the variants. Some single-letter changes are well proven to cause disease, like the one behind sickle cell disease, but for most, statistics is what tells scientists which clues are worth chasing.
